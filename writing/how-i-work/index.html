How I work

A README for working with me: what I will back you on, what I will push back on, and the four things I care about enough to be repetitive about.

Sebastian Tiller August 2026 5 min read

If we end up working together, on my team, as a client, as a partner, or over a coffee where you want an opinion, you will work out my habits eventually. This is an attempt to save you the six months.

I have spent 17 years arguing that enterprise software should not be clunky, confusing, and designed by someone who clearly never has to use it. It seemed only fair to hold the way I work to the same standard. So: the manual. It is a bit dry in places. So am I.

The short version

  • I will back the person before I back the process. When the two are in conflict, the process is where I start looking.
  • The simplest thing that actually works beats the clever thing. Actually works is doing a lot of the lifting in that sentence.
  • I have run remote teams since 2016. Distributed is the default here, not a concession.
  • Family time is sacred. Mine, and yours.
  • The measure I actually care about is whether the team enjoys Monday mornings.

People over process

Great teams build great products, not the other way around.

That is one of the four things I have written on the front of my own website, so you are entitled to hold me to it. In practice it means that when a process and a person are in conflict, my first assumption is that the process is wrong. I am not always right about that. It is still the right place to start looking.

None of which makes me anti-process. I sell process for a living. Octfolio is compliance software for councils, schools, utilities and mining companies, which is to say it is process wearing a login screen. That is exactly why I am careful with it. I have seen what a process looks like when it is designed by someone who never has to live inside it, and I would rather not do that to my own team.

So if something we do internally is making your week worse and it is not protecting anyone, say so. That is not a complaint. That is a bug report.

Elegant solutions

The best answer is usually the simplest one that actually works.

Seven years at Theta taught me that even the most complex business problems can have elegant solutions. It also taught me that the simplest solution which does not work is just a quicker way to be wrong, which is why the second half of that sentence is the half that matters.

Expect me to ask what happens if we do less. It is not a trick and it is not a budget conversation, it is genuinely the question I find most useful. Half the time the answer is that we cannot, and we have lost ten minutes. The other half, we have avoided building something nobody needed.

I came to this sideways. A Bachelor of Interactive Entertainment majoring in animation and special effects, then game QA, then running a photo centre, and then, somehow, solutions architecture. None of it was a plan. What it left me with is a very low tolerance for software that needs a training course before anyone can use it, and a habit of asking who has to hold this thing at six in the morning in the rain.

Remote-first

Talent is everywhere, so why limit yourself to one postcode?

We have been remote since 2016, well before it was fashionable and a good while before the tooling was any good. So I have no romance about it. Remote is not free, it just moves the cost. The friction you would have burned off in a corridor has to be paid for deliberately somewhere else.

The three things that have mattered most are hiring, handover, and the unglamorous habits in between. Hiring, because a distributed team amplifies whoever you let into it. Handover, because work that only exists in one person's head is a single point of failure with a mortgage. The habits, because they are the part everybody skips and then wonders why the whole thing feels harder than it should.

Family time is sacred

No one remembers how late you worked, except your family.

I have built an app whose entire purpose is to stop my kids asking whether we are there yet, which should tell you roughly how my weekends go.

The value cuts both ways, and the second direction is the one that counts. If I am offline, I am offline, and I would like you to be too. A team quietly burning its evenings is not a high-performing team, it is a team with a bill that has not arrived yet.

I will tell you where my interests lie

I run AsbestosList, an independent directory of licensed asbestos professionals across Australia and New Zealand. One of its categories is asbestos management software, which happens to be exactly what Octfolio sells. Competitors are listed too, which is rather the point of calling it independent, and the disclosure sits on the page rather than in a footer where nobody reads it.

The same applies to anything I tell you. If I have a stake in the advice, you will hear it from me before you have to ask.

Still to write

This is a draft, and rather than invent an answer I have left the holes where you can see them. What this document still owes you:

  • How I run one-to-ones, and what the time is actually for.
  • How I give feedback, and how I would like to get it.
  • The real meeting cadence, and what gets written down instead of discussed.
  • Working hours, overlap, and what I do and do not expect after hours.
  • How I hire, and what a first month looks like.
  • How decisions get made when we disagree.

If you have worked with me and think I have described someone else, that is useful information and I would like to hear it.
